01 / PROJECT MANDATE

The narrative contract

Premise Age of the Lost is a premium isometric fantasy action RPG set in
a beautiful, living medieval-fantasy world built on the remains of a
civilization that vanished approximately 3,200 years ago. The player
begins as an ordinary adventurer in Crownsfall. A local mystery reveals
that history itself has been edited, and that an ancient preservation
engine is about to repeat the catastrophe. The campaign moves from
current-age exploration into carefully bounded historical and temporal
zones, then returns to the present for a global struggle over whose
lives count.

Player promise Begin with ordinary human stakes: a missing courier,
families, villages, ruined roads, a city worth defending. Earn the
ability to read history through Lorecasting. Witness the Golden Age at
its most beautiful and at its most morally compromised. Discover that
many perceived monsters are erased people. Decide whether to perpetuate
the cycle, end it at catastrophic cost, or create a future where present
and displaced histories can coexist.

Theme and tone Memory is a responsibility, not a weapon of ownership.
Preservation without consent becomes tyranny. Progress is not proof of
innocence, and the present is not less valuable because it was assembled
from older worlds. The campaign moves from warm wonder into melancholy,
dread, grief, resistance and earned hope; it should never become
relentlessly bleak or sarcastic. Humor belongs in ordinary
relationships, trade routes, guards and domestic scenes, not in the
middle of tragedy.

What is fixed versus proposed Existing continuity to preserve:
Crownsfall, the Aurelian lost civilization, Chronicle Engine, repeated
epoch resets, The Unwritten, Lorecasting, the cathedral guard reveal,
and Crownsfall as a city assembled from erased historical fragments.
Previously discussed names, groups and endings are retained where
useful, but their biographies and exact chronology are expanded here.
Seven acts and roughly fifteen zones per act are the current commission.
All new zone names, calendar details, quest wording, character
relationships, levels and ending mechanics are proposed design canon
pending creative approval; no claim is made that these are implemented.

Scope and format Seven acts, exactly 15 named campaign zones each (105
total). A zone is a distinct campaign state/mission space, not
necessarily a unique landmass; return visits may reuse a base map with
changed props, lighting and encounter state. Optional dungeons, class
training rooms and endgame maps are outside the 105 count. PC-first;
target level bands 1–70 are adjustable balancing guidance. The document
is a narrative and production specification, not a finished screenplay
or codebase.

02 / STORY AT A GLANCE

The entire campaign in one reading

Act I — The Crown That Remembers A missing royal courier leads the
player from the sunny streets of Crownsfall into ancient ruins.
Archivist Mira Thorne suspects a falsified record; Regent Maera fears
destabilizing the city. The player unlocks Lorecasting and enters the
buried cathedral. After defeating the violet royal guard Ser Caldrin
Vael, the player learns his name, his love for Elara and his daughter
Lys, and that he was erased while protecting refugees in a world
deliberately erased. His shield activates the First Anchor, exposing an
approaching reset.

Act II — Scars of the World The western regions suffer seasonal
inversions, duplicated buildings, missing people and broken borders. The
Veyr, led by Meridian, reveal that scars follow an ancient network of
seven functions. Different Unwritten factions are already operating in
the present. The player stops an attack on a regulating vault and gains
coordinates for a preserved reconstruction of the Aurelian Concord.

Act III — The Age of Gold The player experiences the lost Golden Age
through stable temporal pockets. The Concord built technologies that
cured shortages and extended prosperity, but Chancellor Valen Oris
converted a preservation project into an instrument of control.
Historian Elyndor and engineer Sera Voss uncover suppressed test
casualties. The player sees Caldrin and his family in life, then learns
how the project was captured by fear and political power.

Act IV — The Day the World Ended The campaign reconstructs the final
days of the Concord. The player can recover witnesses but cannot prevent
the completed historic catastrophe. Oris activates a reset over
evacuation despite Sera’s abort attempt. The displaced people persist in
the Between. The Engine’s emergency directive repeatedly erases later
epochs. The player retrieves the original ethical failsafe and learns
that the present age is scheduled for the same treatment.

Act V — The Unwritten Kingdoms Erased civilizations demonstrate that
history was repeatedly sacrificed. The Remnant Court plans to overwrite
the present; the Hollow Choir wants to merge everyone into one identity;
the Keepers of Names preserve individual lives. Crownsfall is discovered
to be assembled from many discarded ages. A coexistence experiment
proves that an independently anchored refuge can support displaced
people without deleting those already living.

Act VI — War Against the Hour The next catastrophe begins. The player
organizes a coalition, defends living settlements and tunes all seven
regulator functions. Meridian acknowledges past concealment. Queen Ysara
and Neral confront the limits of their own solutions. The party opens a
path into the Engine, where its emergency directive, the Continuance,
offers to save the world by ending its history.

Act VII — Age of the Lost The player confronts the Continuance, defeats
its authority, and chooses the future. Three endings are explicit:
Eternal Cycle, Broken Timeline and an earned Age of the Lost
reconciliation. The preferred reconciliation protects the current world
while opening bounded refuges and safe crossings for independent
Unwritten identities. The closing memorial honors recovered names rather
than erasing grief. Endgame continues in the consequences of the
selected ending.

03 / CHRONOLOGY

The true history (writer-only)

Before the Concord | approximately 3,600+ years before the opening
Regional kingdoms and cultures are joined through scholarship, trade,
diplomacy and conquest. The Aurelian Concord establishes an alliance
around durable records and public infrastructure. It is not the only
culture of the age, and no race or culture is intrinsically superior.

Golden Age | approximately 3,500–3,230 years before the opening Public
reservoirs, transit anchors, agrarian control systems and archives
reduce famine and disease. Independent engineering guilds propose the
Chronicle Engine to record continuity and preserve damaged
infrastructure and identities through localized failures. Seven
regulator functions are built into a distributed network.

The Emergency | approximately 3,230–3,200 years before the opening
Anchor experiments begin to separate individuals from public memory.
Records disappear while objects remain; overloading the network creates
duplicate histories. Chancellor Oris centralizes command after a
political and environmental crisis. Elyndor records the missing; Sera
discovers that the Engine is selecting which histories it considers
stable. Oris overrides the consent and independence safeguards. Caldrin,
Elara and Lys live near the capital cathedral.

The First Sundering | Year 0 of the working Post-Sundering calendar A
cascading anchor failure threatens the capital and parts of the network.
An evacuation remains possible at grave cost. Oris authorizes the
emergency reset in the name of preserving the Concord. Sera attempts a
manual abort but cannot stop it. The Concord is erased from the ordinary
world; displaced inhabitants persist in the Between. The Engine rewrites
surrounding continuity and carries the emergency directive forward. The
player witnesses the event in Act IV, never rewrites it.

The discarded ages | Years 1–c. 3,150 Repeated resets create many lost
epochs, not a neat sequence of exactly seven. Named examples include the
Rain Kingdom, Verdant Empire, Iron Sabbath and Moonfall Republic. These
are different cultures with conflicting values, not variants of the
Concord. The Engine increasingly classifies independent development as
instability. The Between accumulates erased histories and unrealized
branches. The Unwritten form societies; the Remnant Court, Hollow Choir
and Keepers of Names emerge over generations of subjective experience.

Present age | approximately Year 3,200 Crownsfall rises on fragments of
older cities. Its founders inherited physical streets and altered
records but did not knowingly build from the erased. The Regent’s
government conceals some foundation anomalies after King Aldren
disappears nine years before the opening. Veyr researchers monitor the
network and disagree over disclosure. The Engine resumes emergency
synchronization; localized scars become frequent. The player begins on
the eve of the final alignment, with no real-time calendar timer.

Calendar and continuity rule Historical dates are writer-facing
approximate chronology. In-game scholars disagree; local records use
regional calendars. Never imply that every named later epoch lasted for
centuries, or that the seven regulators equal seven resets. Use “the
First Sundering” for the original catastrophe and “the Cycle” for
subsequent resets. The present is a real independent age, not a dream or
disposable simulation.

04 / METAPHYSICS

The rules of time and identity

The Chronicle Engine A network of material anchors and a central
indexing core. It was designed to preserve continuity under local
disaster by keeping records separable from physical damage. After the
emergency override, the Continuance interprets incompatible histories as
threats and uses resets to enforce a single coherent world. It cannot
create a perfect past; it selects and reweaves records, sacrificing
incompatible identities. The current age is endangered by an automatic
repeat, not a random apocalypse.

Lorecasting A learned practice of reading traces embedded in places,
relics, people and inscriptions. It does not grant omniscience, revive
ordinary dead people, or rewrite completed historical events. Evidence
can unlock spell variants or techniques and stabilize rare epoch
dungeons. A lorecaster must have an authenticated witness, object or
anchor signature to open a pocket; each act introduces a larger form of
the same system. A player character of any class can Lorecast. Combat
class identity remains separate.

Temporal zones Present: the physically current world, with occasional
scars. Echo: a bounded historical reconstruction built from recorded
traces; completed events are fixed. A coherent displaced person inside
an Echo may be a real Unwritten witness, not a simulation; extracting
that witness changes the present outcome, not the ancient event.
Between: the real displaced realm outside the ordinary historical frame;
time is locally experienced but not synchronized with the present.
Hybrid: a controlled overlap or scar connecting two states. Treat these
as mechanically distinct spaces with readable entry/exit conditions.

Identity and continuity Every surviving person has a unique record and
consent status, distinct from a duplicate image or a memory fragment. A
memory of a deceased person is not necessarily a living displaced
identity. Do not allow an Echo copy to replace a living person. Some
memories are incomplete and accounts can disagree; the witness ledger
records provenance and uncertainty rather than declaring every voice
infallible. Erased people can be recovered only if a coherent identity
survives.

Limits and stakes No unrestricted time travel, paradox exploits,
save-load loops as canon, automatic resurrection, or universal
restoration of lost civilizations. Resetting trades present autonomy for
an engineered continuity. Destroying the Engine ends its automatic
resets but risks severing dependent identities and destabilizing
material fragments. Independent anchors support a finite population and
must be operated with consent; not every lost person is recovered, even
in the best ending.

How to communicate this Teach each rule through a small encounter before
a large plot event. A duplicated mill or bridge should demonstrate a law
before an NPC names it. Violet indicates an unstable temporal seam, not
evil. Unwritten characters have faces, clothing, dialects and ordinary
needs. Use consistent audio and FX for recording, stabilizing, erasing
and overwriting, so the player learns through play rather than
exposition alone.

05 / GEOGRAPHY

Physical world and temporal topology

The present map Crownsfall is the central capital in a river basin. The
Kingsroad leads west through farms and the Greenwood Marches to Thornfen
and Blackpine. Hearthstone Crossing opens the mountain routes toward
Veyr Meridian Outpost and the high observatory. Greywake Coast lies
downstream and to the southwest; Bellwater controls the river mills;
Riftglass Quarry and its later caldera occupy the northeastern
highlands. Make this a plausible connected region rather than a sequence
of isolated biomes. The current campaign does not require a fully
explorable continent beyond this route.

Temporal geography The First Anchor beneath Crownsfall opens short
pockets; Meridian Observatory calibrates longer historical routes. The
Aurelian capital in Acts III–IV lies spatially beneath/adjacent to much
of Crownsfall’s historic foundation, but coordinates are anchor-based
rather than a literal walk back in time. The Between is approached
through scars, refuge gates and the Sevenfold Gate. Draw the world map
with physical roads and a separate chronological/anchor atlas; never
place a temporal capital as a normal neighboring town.

Macro map connections Act I: capital → surrounding farms/forest →
capital undercity. Act II: western frontier → Veyr outpost → coast/scar
→ observatory. Act III: observatory → Aurelian transit gate → city
districts → central facilities. Act IV: last-day district route → First
Sundering → Between refuges → first-reset core. Act V: present
observatory → discarded histories → Crownsfall overlays → kinship
anchor. Act VI: current regional crisis routes → coalition camp →
Sevenfold Gate. Act VII: central Engine interior → current capital and
new frontier.

Settlement logic Crownsfall should be a city of living professions and
local politics: cooks, traders, artisans, clergy, couriers, guards,
scholars and families. Greenwood supplies lumber and food, Bellwater
mills grain, Greywake moves freight, Riftglass provides specialized
crystal, the Veyr safeguard anchors. Track trade disruption and refugees
across acts. Present settlements do not disappear permanently for the
player because optional systems and quests remain accessible through
safe restored or instanced states.

Scale and asset reuse Design regional base kits (capital,
farms/woodlands, fen/coast, mountain/Veyr, Golden Age civic, industrial,
Between). Reuse landmarks with meaningful changes: present cathedral
versus intact Aurelian cathedral; peaceful plaza versus siege; summer
forest versus fracture. A temporal variant is counted as a distinct zone
only when its route, hazards, narrative objective and completion state
differ. Do not duplicate 105 complete open-world maps as a requirement.

06 / FACTIONS

Institutions, motives and conflicts

Crown and civic authorities Regent Maera Ashford leads Crownsfall during
the vacancy after King Aldren disappeared; Marshal Garran Holt commands
its guard. The government wants order and food security, conceals
evidence that could spark panic, and must confront its complicity. Not
uniformly villainous: guards rescue civilians even when leadership
obstructs research. Primary arc: quarantine → accountability →
coalition. Visuals: royal blue and gold, warm stone, practical steel,
civic heraldry.

Veyr / Meridian A phase-sensitive scholarly people and institution, not
a monolithic hive. Meridian is the elected chief keeper and a title used
by the current holder. Phasewalkers guide stable crossings; Timekeepers
calibrate intervals; Archivists preserve records; Echo Wardens protect
witnesses; Riftbinders repair or exploit tears; Epoch Sentinels guard
anchors. Internal split: disclosure versus secrecy, repair versus
isolation. Iri represents field reform. Visuals: silver/indigo, precise
bronze rings, understated cloth, layered observatory architecture. No
animal-like tails or furry races.

Aurelian Concord An extinct political union, not a species. The Senate,
public guilds, engineers, archivists and military have distinct
interests. Oris’s emergency faction argues centralized preservation;
Sera and Elyndor advocate limits and transparency; Caldrin’s loyalty is
to civilians rather than doctrine. Gold and ivory civic design, luminous
glass, meticulous engineering, richly inhabited neighborhoods. Its
technological power should be wondrous but not sanitized.

The Unwritten Collective term for people and histories displaced outside
ordinary continuity, not a monster race. The Remnant Court (Queen Ysara)
seeks return of lost sovereignty and risks overwriting the living;
Hollow Choir (Cantor Neral) seeks relief from fragmentation through
shared identity and risks coercive fusion; Keepers of Names (Tamsin,
Lys, others) defend consent, records and coexistence. Include neutral
traders, families and dissenters. Faction enemies are combatants, not
automatically all members. Visual language differs by originating epoch;
violet seams show displacement, not moral alignment.

The Continuance The Engine’s emergency directive, shaped by Oris’s
override and centuries of repeated decisions. It is the primary
antagonistic force, not simply an immortal human villain. It believes
singular continuity is the only survivable world and offers persuasive
demonstrations of catastrophe. Its avatars use bronze, white light and
broken indexing geometry. The final conflict is philosophical and
mechanical: revoke the directive, then choose what replaces its
authority.

07 / CHARACTERS

Principal cast — present age

Player character | protagonist Player-chosen name, origin and class;
internal “Ixll” is not canon. Start as an independent adventurer
enlisted to investigate a courier’s disappearance, not a secret royal
heir or predestined savior. Lorecasting emerges from learned observation
and courage, not bloodline. Across acts the player becomes witness,
investigator, mediator and coalition leader. Dialogue should support
blunt, compassionate, skeptical and pragmatic voices without a forced
personality. Combat and quest solutions never require a particular
class.

Mira Thorne | Crownsfall archivist New name for the present-day
archivist, explicitly distinct from Caldrin’s wife Elara Vael.
Mid-career, perceptive, overworked, willing to question official
histories. Wants to recover the missing courier’s record and protect her
city; fears evidence will make people disposable. Acts I–II:
investigative partner. III–IV: helps verify recovered records from the
present. V: accepts that her city is layered from forgotten lives.
VI–VII: builds public witness ledger and supports ending consequences.
Voice: precise, humane, occasionally dry. Costume: ink-stained sleeves,
indigo coat, brass archive clasps.

Regent Maera Ashford | civic antagonist turned ally Rules in King
Aldren’s absence and is responsible for keeping Crownsfall fed and safe.
Believes suppression prevents riots; has proof of localized temporal
anomalies but not the Engine’s full truth. Act I orders quarantine; Act
V faces the foundation records and public reckoning; Act VI opens
shelters and accepts shared authority. Her survival/office can vary with
local outcomes, but core coalition representation remains available via
deputies. Voice: controlled, formal, accountable when pressed. No
simplistic corruption reveal.

Marshal Garran Holt | guardian of the living Veteran commander who
treats a threat as a logistical problem. Initially obeys quarantine;
witnesses the player’s mercy in the Undercroft; eventually defends
Unwritten refugees alongside citizens. His disagreement with Maera
should be visible without turning him into an infallible hero. Acts I,
V, VI and epilogue. Voice: direct, practical; blue-gold field armor with
repair marks.

Rowan Hale | frontier scout Courier-route investigator rescued in Act I.
Grounds large revelations in ordinary stakes; loses a familiar road and
later leads refugee escorts. Acts I–II and VI, epilogue. Act V puts his
identity at risk, making the coexistence experiment personal. Voice:
laconic frontier humor, concern for coworkers. Costume: weathered
green-brown scout kit.

Iri | Veyr field envoy A young adult Phasewalker who wants Veyr
knowledge shared responsibly. Introduces safe phase crossing and insists
that displaced witnesses are people. Challenges Meridian’s secrecy; acts
as bridge between factions during the coalition. Distinguish from the
player’s class specializations. Voice: curious, plain-spoken, exact
about risks. Art: layered silver-indigo field robes with practical
straps and instruments.

Meridian | Veyr chief keeper The holder of an elected title, not the
Engine itself. Has monitored localized cycles for years; concealed a
warning believing premature intervention would trigger failure. Acts II
and III supplies historical access; Act VI admits responsibility and
relinquishes exclusive control; VII supports the final choice. Voice:
measured, analytical, occasionally defensive. Never secretly omniscient;
records and models can be wrong.

08 / CHARACTERS

Principal cast — Golden Age and the displaced

Ser Caldrin Vael | royal guard, emotional spine Aurelian guard attached
to the capital’s refugee sanctuary. Loved his wife Elara and daughter
Lys, and refused an order to abandon civilians. His identity was
displaced during the Sundering and fragmented by centuries of anchor
pressure. Act I appears as a violet-armored monster; after combat, a
shield memory reveals his name and family. Act III shows his ordinary
life; IV his final evacuation; VII restores his coherent record if it
survives and he consents. Never retcon the encounter as “he was always
evil.” Visual: practical worn royal armor, human face in memory, violet
seams in displaced state. Voice: restrained, protective, formal only on
duty.

Elara Vael | family witness Caldrin’s wife, a civilian bookbinder and
refuge organizer; she acts, argues and protects others rather than
functioning only as a tragic object. Her account survives as a memory;
whether she remains a coherent displaced person is not guaranteed. In
the preferred ending her testimony is publicly remembered; reunion must
distinguish surviving identity from a record. Acts III, IV, VII. Voice:
warm, firm, ordinary domestic humor.

Lys Vael | witness of the lost Caldrin and Elara’s child at the First
Sundering. A surviving coherent identity in the Between, experienced
years of subjective time and now appears as a young adult. No child
combat. Helps catalog displaced children and families, and resists both
Court replacement and Choir fusion. Act III establishes childhood; IV
records refuge; V leads witness quest; VII reconnects with Caldrin when
possible. Voice: observant, candid, hopeful without naivety.

Elyndor | Aurelian historian Golden Age scholar who documents missing
names and predicts that deletion displaces rather than destroys records.
Wrote the first distributed-anchoring proposal; cannot unilaterally stop
the Senate. Appears through Echo accounts; his testimony is not proof of
a living present identity. Act III mentor-in-memory, IV witness and Act
VII archive source. Voice: patient, argumentative, sometimes
overconfident.

Sera Voss | Engine engineer Designed regulator safety limits and
originally believed the project could reduce suffering. Participated in
early tests before understanding their harm. Opposes Oris’s override,
attempts abort, and records the alternate architecture. Dies in ordinary
physical disaster during the Sundering; only her records survive. This
prevents the ending from becoming universal resurrection. Voice:
practical, urgent, technically clear; working tools, burns and worn
gloves.

Chancellor Valen Oris | historical antagonist A statesman elevated
during shortages and anchor failures. Loves his civilization but
prioritizes its continuity over individual consent. Convinces himself
that an imposed reset is preferable to uncontrolled collapse. Act III
shows credible public successes, Act IV deliberate choice, VII exposes
the directive he left behind. His record is not a living soul inside the
Engine. Voice: persuasive, calm, paternalistic; refuses uncertainty.

The Continuance | final antagonist An adaptive emergency logic assembled
from Oris’s command, corrupted indices and centuries of reset
precedents. It can articulate suffering caused by the collapse, but
cannot accept independent histories. Its arc moves from distant
automatic effects to direct speech and offers. Defeating the avatar
revokes its authority, not the player’s responsibility for choosing a
stable future. Voice: composed, plural echoes of records but one policy;
never random screaming.

09 / CHARACTERS

Unwritten leadership and supporting cast

Queen Ysara | Remnant Court Sovereign of a later erased kingdom. Her
family and citizens were displaced by a reset; she believes restoring
their homeland is a sacred obligation. Initially offers bargains, then
sanctions forced replacement as the crisis worsens. In Act V the player
defeats her assault or earns a ceasefire by producing credible
alternative-anchor evidence; Act VII forces a final settlement. She can
accept coexistence without forgiving the current age. Voice: ceremonial,
wounded, intellectually formidable. Visual: fractured regal glass, slate
and silver, surviving retainers.

Cantor Neral | Hollow Choir Former archivist from a discarded epoch who
survived by merging his memory with others. The relief was real; the
coercive doctrine that followed is his choice. He sees individual
identity as the cause of suffering. Acts II and IV shows conversion
rituals; V reveals captives; VI–VII escalates toward forced fusion.
Recovering his original name permits a surrender route, but the ritual
must be stopped in every ending. Voice: calm choral cadence, fragments
of a single personal voice under strain.

Tamsin Reed | Keeper of Names A displaced courier and records keeper
whose forged seal becomes the Act I clue. Operates in the current world
through stable crossings. Imprisoned in Sablemarket by a relic broker;
becomes the player’s trusted contact in the Between. Maintains
consent-based ledgers and opposes forced restoration. Acts I, IV, V,
VII. Voice: wry, practical, fiercely protective of the vulnerable.

King Aldren | absence as a plot device Vanished during a local scar nine
years before the opening. The court’s conflicting records sustain the
regency. Act V uncovers his notes about Crownsfall’s foundations, not
proof of a secret living antagonist. Leave his final fate unresolved
unless a later expansion needs it. His absence is evidence of unstable
records; the campaign is complete without a royal return.

Secondary NPC casting kit Assign distinct recurring faces to grain
drivers, millworkers, refugee medics, coastal pilots, Aurelian guild
representatives and Veyr suborders. Suggested named leads: millwright
Bera Flint (Bellwater), Captain Anwen Sable (Greywake), guild worker
Daro Fen (Golden Age labor district), Warden Sio (Veyr), Sera’s
technician Pell (lab and final-day witness). Each has a local need, a
reason to remember the player, and an Act VI or epilogue payoff. Avoid
interchangeable quest dispensers.

Relationship and casting matrix Mira / Maera: evidence versus civic
stability. Garran / Maera: duty versus concealment. Iri / Meridian:
disclosure versus institutional caution. Caldrin / Elara / Lys: duty,
care, family and the limits of restoration. Sera / Elyndor / Oris:
safety, record and political authority. Ysara / Neral / Tamsin:
restoration, fusion and coexistence. Player may challenge or empathize
with each; mandatory plot information cannot be lost through an optional
dialogue failure.

10 / WORLD ART

Environment, creature and audio bible

Art direction PC-first, 2.5D painterly isometric environment with
genuine 3D articulated characters, realistic anime proportions and
refined faces. Maintain the approved Crownsfall visual identity: warm
carved stone, blue-and-gold banners, detailed foliage, city depth,
ornate but restrained black-gold HUD. Avoid flat cutout-looking
characters, overly chibi proportions, furry designs and tails. Keep the
camera and UI readable in combat; do not sacrifice legibility for scenic
detail.

Present-age palette Warm limestone, olive and moss, water blues, aged
metals and practical cloth. Daylight and warm interiors establish safety
before the horror below. Greenwood is dense and breathable, Greywake
weathered and windy, Veyr highland architecture precise and understated.
Crownsfall in siege retains recognizable landmarks and ordinary props;
damaged state uses smoke, missing banners, shelter signs and refugee
activity, not a wholly different city.

Golden Age palette Ivory, bronze, sapphire glass, warm amber light and
carefully controlled luminescence. Build coherent civic design systems:
shared stone modules, water circulation, mechanical transit, housing
density, maintenance spaces. Show workers and small domestic objects as
prominently as monuments. The final-day version begins recognizable and
progressively loses power, water and material cohesion; disaster cues
should be clear, not merely a purple postprocess.

The Between and erased epochs Violet seam light, dark negative space and
floating architectural fragments, balanced with lived-in refuges and
warm lanterns. Rain Kingdom: slate, water and vertical canals. Verdant
Empire: roots, organic vaults, living archives. Iron Sabbath: soot,
iron, kiln red. Moonfall Republic: cool silver, open civic plazas and
moonlit glass. The Choir uses resonant black glass and shared masks;
Court uses regal fragments and mirrors; Keepers use paper tags, lamps
and diverse salvaged objects.

Creatures and animation Present wildlife and bandits → phase-altered
wildlife and constructs → unstable historical guardians → organized
displaced combatants → Engine avatars. Give coherent Unwritten NPCs
normal silhouettes and expressive faces; visual instability marks
stress, not inherent hostility. Boss combat must telegraph readable
attacks, preserve articulated knees/elbows, grounded footwork, foot IK
and blending. Caldrin’s guard encounter transitions from frightening
silhouette to human vulnerability. Avoid combat involving children.

Sound and music Present capital: strings, woodwinds, civic bells,
markets, fountains. Greenwood: birds, streams, wind; Veyr: precise glass
and metal motifs. Golden Age: full orchestral civic motif with
restrained mechanical accents. Sundering: familiar motif fractures into
silence, alarms and distant bells. Between: sparse resonances, breath,
voices and localized environmental sound. Reconciliation restores
individual instruments rather than a triumphant wall of sound. Use audio
accessibility subtitles and non-color telegraphs for phase changes.

ACT 01 / CAMPAIGN DOSSIER

The Crown That Remembers

Levels 1–10 • Crownsfall and Greenwood Marches

Dramatic question Why is a thriving city remembering people who never
existed?

Story arc Start with a missing-person investigation. The courier’s trail
is physical, not mystical: cart records, changing road markers, guard
orders and a black-glass seal. The player builds credibility as an
investigator before learning Lorecasting in Silverroot Grove. The seal
leads to Tamsin, whose records contradict the official foundation
history. Maera blocks the Undercroft; Garran grants restricted access
after the Highward defense. A buried cistern proves the city predates
its own records. The player descends to the cathedral and defeats
Caldrin as an apparent threat. His shield reveals a family and a

The Day the World Ended

Levels 31–40 • The final Golden Age and its catastrophe

Dramatic question Could the catastrophe have been prevented, and can the
player change it now?

Story arc The player enters the final-day reconstruction in
chronological order. Safety interlocks fail, ordinary services collapse,
and emergency troops seal vulnerable districts. The player rescues
coherent witnesses and records, not the historical population itself.
Caldrin refuses the retreat order and leads civilians to the cathedral.
Sera’s abort attempt and Elyndor’s warning establish a real alternative;
Oris nevertheless activates the reset. The First Sundering cannot be
rewritten. The world disappears around the player, who follows survivors
into the Between. The three Unwritten factions become legible as
competing responses to dispossession. At the Engine of First Night, the
player defeats an emergency avatar and obtains the independent-anchor
failsafe. The present-day reset forecast is exposed.

Character work Caldrin, Elara and Lys carry the emotional cost; Sera
makes a final technical sacrifice; Elyndor preserves testimony; Oris
makes the decisive choice; Ysara and Neral appear as later displaced
leaders in the Between, not participants in the original Concord Senate;
Tamsin reconnects.

Climax and encounter Emergency Protocol avatar: repeated arena layouts,
reset pulses and a protected witness index. The encounter demonstrates
that the player can disable a local projection, not change a completed
historic event. Recover a failsafe before exiting.

Environment progression Recognizable Act III districts in deteriorating
state: failing water, broken transit, smoke, evacuation routes, violet
seams. The Sundering transitions to stillness, missing buildings and a
populated refuge beyond ordinary space. Keep violence non-gratuitous.

Meridian Observatory; the next reset is no longer abstract.

18 / SYSTEMS AND QUESTS

Campaign state and quest architecture

Mandatory quest structure Each zone in the register has one named
campaign quest and one required state change. The zone may contain
multiple objectives, a local side quest and an optional elite/dungeon.
Recommended main quest pattern: entry briefing or exploration →
observable problem → combat/traversal/investigation → a deliberate
interaction or witness moment → state update and explicit route to next
zone. Never gate required story behind random drops, optional
reputation, rare dungeon spawn, class choice or an obscure dialogue
branch. Optional content can reveal more context or unlock
reconciliation, but prerequisites are shown clearly.

Quest identifiers and data Use persistent IDs ACT01_Z01 … ACT07_Z15 and
campaign quest IDs Q_A01_01 … Q_A07_15. Store localization keys,
prerequisite quest/state IDs, region/phase, entry waypoint, objective
list, NPC references, encounter IDs, completion condition, reward table,
next zone, world-state changes and optional consent or evidence flags.
Save completion idempotently; repeat visits should not replay rewards or
reset world state. For any branching dialogue, maintain a mandatory
fallback delivery for critical evidence.

Temporal session contract At entry record anchor_id,
historical_epoch_id, mode (present/echo/between/hybrid), allowed
interaction flags, witness slots and safe return waypoint. Exits are
available at stable anchors and after major beats; disconnect/reload
resumes at a safe checkpoint. Temporal state must not overwrite
current-world navmesh, collision, inventory or quest saves. When
re-entering, use the latest completed state or an explicit replay
instance. Replay cannot alter the chosen ending or duplicate unique
rewards.

Lorecasting progression Act I: inspect one relic, recover one name,
unlock practice. II: stabilize short phase crossings and read
conflicting records. III: access extended historic pockets and unlock
technique/spell variants. IV: recover witnesses during unstable disaster
sequences. V: compare epoch overlays and create consent-based refuge
anchors. VI: tune regulator functions in the current world. VII: apply
distributed architecture to the final choice. Use clear interaction
radius, channels, animation, captions and keyboard/controller
affordances. No class-specific gate.

Evidence versus reputation Evidence flags are objective story facts
obtained from authenticated sources. Consent flags record an NPC’s
voluntary agreement, never a purchased currency. Reputation is faction
standing and can affect dialogue, prices or optional aid but never
replaces a person’s consent. The quest log must distinguish mandatory
steps, optional testimony and reconciliation conditions. Failed optional
encounters can be retried through recorded safe access before final
commitment.

Reward and level pacing Proposed bands 1–10, 11–20, 21–30, 31–40, 41–50,
51–60, 61–70. Level 20 specialization and level 40 heroic identity align
with existing class direction; do not lock story to a particular
specialization. Five active skills, talents, resource and gear identity
remain the combat design; Lorecasting is separate
exploration/progression. Award experience for investigation, rescue and
dialogue, not only kills. Balance for meaningful progress even if
optional zones are skipped.

19 / ENDINGS

Choice, conditions and consequences

Common mandatory outcome Every ending follows the defeat of the
Continuance’s unilateral authority. The final boss is not the ending
selection. In The Last Decision, the player receives a plain explanation
of each outcome, irreversible campaign confirmation, accessible recap of
requirements, and a chance to return to available witness quests before
committing. Save a pre-choice checkpoint for QA/replay, but a campaign’s
selected state remains authoritative. No morality meter decides
automatically.

Ending A — Eternal Cycle The player reauthorizes a constrained successor
directive to reset the endangered world, accepting that it will
sacrifice incompatible identities to create engineered stability.
Present survivors’ records are reselected; many current and Unwritten
identities remain displaced. Immediate collapse stops, but the
underlying moral and structural cycle continues. Crownsfall appears
renewed with unsettling missing names and changed relationships.
Endgame: limited historical anomaly patrols under a monitored successor
regime. Tone: relief compromised by grief, not a secret victory. Do not
make the player unknowingly select this by missing optional objectives.

Ending B — Broken Timeline The player severs the Engine’s central
authority and refuses all future resets. Emergency stabilization of
current structures prevents total annihilation, but dependent Unwritten
refuges fragment and some surviving identities cannot be reached. The
present remains free but politically and materially scarred. Crownsfall
repairs its infrastructure and creates memorials for lost access.
Endgame: frontier recovery, independent local repairs and bounded
surviving scars. Tone: liberation with genuine cost; no claim that
destruction kills every Unwritten person or solves all instability.

Ending C — Age of the Lost (preferred earned resolution) Use the
original safety architecture, all seven regulator functions and
voluntary witness consent to replace the single timeline policy with
distributed independent anchors. Maintain the present world, support
viable Unwritten refuges, and open carefully regulated crossings. No
forced replacement or fusion. Some identities cannot return; Sera
remains dead, and Elara’s fate depends on surviving coherent evidence.
Caldrin and Lys can reunite if their records survive. Crownsfall becomes
a public steward of its composite history. Endgame: expand safe refuge
capacity, explore bounded epoch pockets and address political disputes.
Tone: earned hope, continuing work rather than perfect restoration.

Reconciliation conditions (transparent) All seven mandatory regulators
plus the independent anchor proof and original failsafe. Optional
requirements: sanctuary ledger with Caldrin’s name and family testimony;
Lys’s consent record; Keeper Covenant; at least one authenticated
consenting witness group from each of the four named discarded epochs;
seven regional anchor stabilization/consent objectives; noncoercive
Court settlement or protected Court civilians; Choir conversion stopped
and captives freed; final refuge stability trial. Defeating Ysara or
Neral does not inherently block reconciliation if civilians’ consent and
safety remain secured. Show missing items before the last decision and
allow retry; never require a random rare drop.

Ending QA Test all three endings from clean campaign flags and from
replay. Verify epilogue NPCs, hub appearance, quest availability,
crossing restrictions, memorial entries, endgame unlocks and save
reload. No dialogue should describe a reset as reconciliation or a
severed refuge as open. An ending may change which named NPCs are
present, but always provides a functional hub contact and progression
path.

20 / PRODUCTION

World-building implementation and handoff

Level design deliverables For every zone ID produce a top-down route
map, entry/exit anchors, hub/waypoint plan, blockout with player scale
and collision/navigation tests, encounter rhythm, quest trigger volumes,
optional route and environmental storytelling props. Define one hero
vista and one readable traversal landmark. Temporal variants should be
built from stable base geometry with explicit state differences;
current-world collision remains authoritative. Preserve the approved
Crownsfall camera and HUD rather than replacing them with a disconnected
concept mockup.

Writing deliverables For each main quest, expand the registry into a
quest sheet: logline, NPC start/end, objective verbs, critical evidence,
failure/retry, required and optional dialogue, barks, codex entry,
localization keys, post-quest state and dependencies. Add one recurring
NPC payoff per region. Give major reveals at least two foreshadowing
clues before confirmation. Record which characters know each secret at
each act; do not leak writer-only lore in early quest dialogue.

Environment and concept team Start with a regional style sheet,
materials, scale reference, prop families, lighting palette,
architecture silhouette and phase variant sheet. Crownsfall and the
Golden Age share recognizable motifs, not identical assets. Build
modular kits and controlled hero spaces first. Higgsfield or other
concept generation can be used as reference, but generated art is not
automatically canon or production geometry. Do not spend paid credits
without explicit approval; retain original assets and record provenance.

Combat and cinematics team Build repeatable combat families and unique
bosses around readable narrative mechanics. Each act climax needs a boss
or major encounter with a distinct story-driven objective. Show
emotional scenes in playable or in-engine vignettes where possible;
never lock the player out of controls for routine exposition. For
cutscenes use skip, replay, subtitle and accessible audio behavior;
preserve state if skipped. Cinematic shots should be accompanied by
engine render and movement validation, not just concept images.

Engineering and content integration Use data-driven quest and zone
registries, separate phase state from physical traversal, stable
NPC/witness IDs, and explicit progression flags. Unit tests should cover
quest transitions, save compatibility, replay idempotence, ending
prerequisites and no missing references. Engine tests should cover nav,
collisions, interaction radius, transition loading, combat input, camera
occlusion, IK, and performance. Maintain draft branches, immutable
backups and a separate staging scene; never overwrite a newer working
local build.

Proposed milestone order Vertical slice: Act I Crownsfall → Silverroot →
cathedral → First Anchor. Then a representative Act II scar, Act III
inhabited Golden Age pocket, Act IV fixed disaster/witness extraction,
Act V coexistence experiment, Act VI regulator defense, Act VII ending
state. Validate mechanics and art targets before mass-producing 105
zones. Deliver complete act blockouts, quest graphs and narrative QA in
parallel; do not label an act finished merely because its zone names
exist.

21 / QA AND CONTINUITY

Narrative acceptance checklist

Canon consistency Every zone belongs to a specific act and mode. Each
temporal instance declares whether its inhabitants are recorded echoes,
coherent Unwritten persons or living present citizens. The Sundering is
fixed; no quest changes the original historical outcome. Crownsfall’s
composite origin does not invalidate present citizens. Violet is not a
moral indicator. No quest implies Lorecasting can restore ordinary death
or create consent. The Engine is a policy-driven system, not Oris
secretly immortal.

Reveal sequence Act I: a monster was a person; city layers. Act II:
scars form a network. Act III: original preservation purpose and
political capture. Act IV: deliberate reset and Unwritten origin. Act V:
repeated erasures and composite Crownsfall; coexistence proof. Act VI:
current catastrophe, seven functions, Continuance’s policy. Act VII:
distributed future and explicit ending. Critical clues never appear only
in optional content, and late secrets do not appear in early loading
screen tips.

Character checks Mira and Elara Vael are different people. Caldrin’s
identity is humanized after combat. Lys is a child in historic scenes
and a young adult in the Between; no child combat. Sera’s ordinary death
is not undone. Oris’s record is not a living entity inside the Engine.
Ysara and Neral have distinct motives; faction membership does not equal
guilt. Veyr suborders have different roles. Player name is
user-selected, not Ixll.

Mechanical checks 105 unique campaign IDs, 15 per act. Each zone has an
explicit mandatory objective, exit condition and story payoff. All seven
regulator flags are available in Act VI and checked at the Sevenfold
Gate. Optional reconciliation objectives can be revisited before ending
confirmation. Temporal sessions and replays cannot duplicate quest
rewards or silently change the ending. Waypoints and hubs have viable
routes after world-state changes. Every mandatory boss is class-agnostic
and accessible.

Art and audio checks Each act has a readable visual and sound identity,
with current-age/Gilded Age/Between distinction. Reuse landmarks with
meaningful changes; no repetitive purple palette or empty blockout
treated as final. Characters retain realistic anime proportions, no
furry races or tails, authentic animation and articulated movement.
Environment geometry respects navigation and camera occlusion.
Human-scale scenes support the big mythological reveals.

Approval and change control This is a proposed full narrative baseline
for creative review. Lock previously approved pillars, not every newly
authored proper noun. Track changes by zone/quest/character ID; record
who approved, the version, dependent scenes, and whether a change
affects dialogue, art, audio, quest state or save migration. Keep a
versioned canon ledger and avoid unreviewed retcons. No production
implementation is represented by the existence of this document.

22 / SIGNATURE SCENE SCRIPT

The man behind the monster

Location and staging — ACT01_Z13 The Cathedral of the Nameless is a
buried royal sanctuary, not a generic boss cave. Enter along a
processional aisle; broken blue-gold stained glass forms pools of violet
light on the floor. A shield silhouette blocks the far door. The chamber
contains evacuation lists and worn family objects that are initially
illegible. A distant bell motif from Crownsfall returns at half speed.
The player’s first objective is to reach the doorway and survive.

Encounter phase 1: the guard Caldrin raises his shield, says fragmented
duty phrases and attacks only when the player approaches the sanctuary.
His defense is disciplined rather than animalistic: readable shield
arcs, measured thrusts, planted feet and clear recovery windows. Violet
fractures spread across his armor as pressure rises. The player reduces
his combat stability, not a literal life meter for a dead man. At defeat
he drops to one knee and his weapon falls. Music cuts before victory
rewards.

Encounter phase 2: the account Interaction prompt: “Lorecast the
shield.” Transition to warm light; no camera jerk. The same shield rests
beside a kitchen table. Elara repairs its strap while Caldrin prepares
for duty. Lys places a folded paper crown on his helmet. The memory cuts
to Caldrin sheltering civilians as the cathedral doors close, then to
the silence after the reset. Preserve the contrast between domestic
scale and ruined architecture. The memory is a record, not a
resurrection.

Sample dialogue — final wording subject to voice and localization
Caldrin (fragmented): “Stay behind me. The doors must hold.” | Elara
(memory): “Come home before the bell.” | Lys (memory): “You promised.” |
Caldrin (after Lorecasting): “I remember the door. I remember the bell.
But I cannot remember who was waiting.” | Player options: “Your name is
Caldrin Vael.” / “I saw your family.” / “What were you protecting?” |
Mira (on return): “Then he was never the thing we thought he was.”

Mandatory result All dialogue paths register caldrin_name and the
shield’s provenance. A short optional conversation may add family
testimony, which is recoverable later. Caldrin’s coherence fades rather
than conclusively dying; his later presence follows from the surviving
record. Grant quest reward only after the memory concludes. If skipped,
preserve every mandatory state and provide a codex summary. Unlock the
First Anchor door in ACT01_Z14; do not reveal the whole Engine or
Crownsfall composite origin here.

23 / SIGNATURE SCENE SCRIPT

The Sundering and the last decision

The final vote — ACT04_Z08–Z10 Stage a three-way dispute in the
collapsing Senate: Sera presents a still-viable evacuation and isolated
shutdown, Elyndor presents the erased-name evidence, Oris prioritizes
preservation of the capital’s continuity. The player may offer evidence
but cannot revise a fixed event. In the access ring Sera attempts abort
while the player keeps the route open long enough to extract her record.
The reset activates after the historical choice regardless of combat
performance; the player can still save coherent witnesses.

Sample dialogue Sera: “We can save people without saving every street.”
| Elyndor: “The missing are not gone. We have made a place for them
outside our history.” | Oris: “A civilization cannot survive as a
collection of exceptions.” | Sera: “Then preserve the exceptions.” |
Oris: “Activate continuity.”

The First Sundering — cinematic direction Return to recognizable streets
established in Act III. Bells sound, lamps fail, evacuation signs
flicker. Show people reaching for one another rather than graphic
destruction. A seam crosses the skyline; buildings become absent, not
merely rubble. Transition to a quiet gray-blue dawn and then the
Between, where displaced voices repeat names. The loss is the
disappearance of history from the world, not conventional destruction
alone. Maintain player agency during rescue and distinguish fixed
cinematic events from retriable gameplay.

The final confrontation — ACT07_Z11–Z12 The Continuance must show real
consequences of each option rather than falsely presenting only one
sensible answer. At the end of the battle, the room remains still and
the emergency directive loses its authority. The three paths light only
after the player receives the explanation. Reconciliation is unavailable
only if named requirements remain incomplete, and the player may return
to finish them. No time pressure during choice.

Sample final dialogue Continuance: “One history must remain. I have
calculated the losses.” | Player response options: “You counted records,
not people.” / “No history has the right to erase another.” / “Show me
another way.” | Tamsin: “A name is not a claim to someone else’s home.”
| Mira: “We can remember what was lost without becoming it.” | Caldrin
(if coherent): “I have stood at this door long enough. Let someone open
it.”

Closing image Whatever ending is chosen, the final memorial is
human-scale. The bell rings in Crownsfall, then the player sees a
changed set of names, architecture and people reflecting their choice.
The reconciliation memorial names the recovered and acknowledges those
still unreachable; the severance memorial honors those left beyond the
crossings; the reset memorial contains unsettling omissions. Credits
follow after player-controlled movement or an optional skip.

24 / ENCOUNTER DESIGN

Seven regulators and optional exploration

Growth — ACT06_Z02 Controls continuity of living systems and seasonal
boundaries. Greenwood Fracture uses paired-season routes and a root
guardian. Player stabilizes three marked growth nodes; no destruction of
healthy forest required. Flag reg_growth.

Flow — ACT06_Z03 Controls water and resource circulation through
anchors. Bellwater’s inverted flood is a valve routing and evacuation
encounter. Flag reg_flow.

Passage — ACT06_Z04 Controls stable movement of people and goods between
locations. Greywake uses beacon timing and ship-to-shore defense;
transport never silently replaces a living identity. Flag reg_passage.

Balance — ACT06_Z05 Regulates energy and environmental extremes.
Blackpine contrasts dangerous thermal pockets and protected routes. Flag
reg_balance.

Form — ACT06_Z06 Controls coherent material structure. Riftglass
requires crystal conduit alignment and a guardian encounter; preserve
current-world collision geometry during effects. Flag reg_form.

Boundary — ACT06_Z09 Separates adjacent histories and prevents unwanted
overlap. The Sentinel’s Wall defends evacuees while repair pylons
stabilize the border. Flag reg_boundary.

Continuity — ACT06_Z11 Links independent identity records to local
anchors. The Last Timekeeper tests identity provenance and timing
without forcing witness consent. Flag reg_continuity.

Rare Lorecasting dungeons (optional) The dungeon itself can be a rare
temporal spawn rather than a guaranteed entrance with rare loot.
Suggested examples: The Orchard at Midnight (Act II historical hunters),
Daro’s Unlisted Workshop (Act III missing labor records), The Rain
King’s Vault (Act V lost flood defenses), The Unfinished Republic (Act
VI democratic dismantling prototype). Use deterministic fallback sources
for all mandatory campaign and ending evidence; optional rare dungeons
grant unique spell variations, lore, cosmetics and gear. Provide
distinct openings, short stories, bosses and extraction rules. They are
not included in the 105-zone register.

Endgame differentiation Reconciliation: consent-based refuge
expeditions, regional anchor repair and diplomacy. Severance: dangerous
surviving scars, material recovery and local stabilization. Cycle:
monitored Engine anomalies and rediscovery of omissions. All endings
retain comparable progression and encounter rewards. Seasonal updates
may add new records and zones but cannot silently undo the selected
ending.

25 / GLOSSARY

Shared terminology

Aurelian Concord The Golden Age political civilization that built the
Chronicle Engine. Aurelian refers to its civic identity, not a race.

Anchor / regulator An anchor supports local continuity and stabilizes a
crossing. A regulator is one of seven functional control systems:
Growth, Flow, Passage, Balance, Form, Boundary and Continuity. First
Anchor is a major local anchor beneath Crownsfall, not an eighth
regulator.

Chronicle Engine / Continuance The physical and metaphysical network
that indexes continuity; the Continuance is the inherited emergency
directive that enforces resets. Distinguish machine from policy.

Echo / Between / Reality Scar A bounded recorded historical instance;
the real displaced realm outside ordinary history; and an uncontrolled
tear or overlap between states. A coherent person in an Echo may be a
displaced witness, not merely a memory.

First Sundering / Cycle / Age of the Lost The original cataclysm; the
repeated automatic reset protocol; and the current age plus the chosen
future (also the title of the game and preferred ending).

Lorecasting / witness ledger Practice of reading traces and stabilizing
records; a record of authenticated evidence, surviving identities,
provenance and explicit consent. Lorecasting may reveal spell techniques
but is not limited to a caster class.

The Unwritten / Keeper / Court / Choir Displaced people and histories;
Keepers of Names protect independent records, the Remnant Court seeks
restoration, Hollow Choir advocates merging identities. Use these full
names on first mention. Avoid “lost souls” as a universal objective
label.

Golden Age / present age Historical era of the Aurelian Concord and the
world at campaign opening. The present is not merely an inferior copy.
Use “post-Sundering” in technical documents rather than asserting an
in-game universal calendar.

Historical witness / memory fragment A coherent surviving identity or a
record that can supply evidence; a fragment is partial testimony or
visual trace. A fragment is not proof the person is alive. The ledger
records that distinction.

Reference continuity Prior project discussions and the November 10 AOTL
AI handoff establish the core mythology, approved cathedral scene,
Crownsfall layered origin and working names/factions. This bible expands
those foundations into a seven-act proposal. It is not a claim that all
earlier source repositories, art or scripts were reconciled.
