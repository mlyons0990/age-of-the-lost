# Age of the Lost — the campaign

Rewritten 2026-11-28 from the owner's story brief. Seven acts, about fifteen zones each, alternating between the
present day and the past. Zone rules from the blueprint addendum (§2.8–2.9) still apply: PoE zone model, at least
3 x 3 tiles per wild zone, several points of interest per wild zone, every zone blends into the next, every zone
is built from a reusable kit.

Status: **built** = playable (greybox on the forest kit until its own art is approved) · **planned** = not built.

---

## The world: Aethryn

**Aethryn** is the world (as Azeroth or Wraeclast). The name is old Aurelian for "the course of the ages".

- **The Gilded Epoch** was the golden age. It was ruled by **the Aurelian Concord**, a civilisation of chronomancers,
  paladins, dragon-riders and sky-ships. Magic was ordinary and myths walked the land.
- **The Sundering of Hours** was the temporal cataclysm that ended it in a single night. Cities were folded out of
  time and the Concord vanished. What was left was buried, drowned or frozen into the land.
- **The Age of the Lost** is the present, thousands of years later. People know only that a magnificent age once
  existed. Crownsfall calls itself "the last free city" without remembering what it was free *of*. Everyone is
  living in the Age of the Lost.
- **Temporal rifts** are now opening, and things from other times bleed through them: Concord wardens, extinct
  beasts, clockwork soldiers, myths. Behind them all is something that stands outside time.
- **The hero, the Wayfarer**, can pass through the rifts and comes back unchanged. That should be impossible. They
  travel between the present, the timeless **Hourless Deep** and the past, to learn what ended the Gilded Epoch and
  whether the present can be saved.

### The truth, revealed across the acts

1. The Concord built **the Aeon Engine** to master time. It worked, and through it they saw what waits beyond time:
   **the Unwritten**, a hunger that eats histories.
2. The Concord's ruler, **Archon Celestine Vael**, chose to hide the world from the Unwritten. She would fold the
   Gilded Epoch out of time and leave an unremarkable, "lost" age behind that the Unwritten would pass over. That
   choice was the Sundering.
3. It half worked. The Unwritten was blinded but not stopped. Now the Wayfarer's journeys through the rifts are
   wearing the fold thin, and the rifts follow them.
4. The Wayfarer was born of the fold itself: a person from neither age. That is why the rifts do not unmake them,
   and why they alone can make the final choice.

### The ending choice (Act VII)

| Ending | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Restore the Gilded Epoch** | Undo the Sundering. The golden age returns, the Age of the Lost never happens, and the Wayfarer's present is erased. |
| **Preserve the Age of the Lost** | Seal the fold for good. The past stays lost, the present survives, and the rifts close. |
| **Forge the Braided Age** | Weave both times into one, something never done before. Both ages live, and the Unwritten must be faced in the open. This is the hardest path, and the bridge to post-campaign play. |

## Lorecasting

Secret lorecast sites hold the remains or echo of a historic figure or event. Finding one, and usually defeating
its reanimated echo, teaches a **lorecast skill** usable by any class. Examples: a Mage learns Radiant Spear from an
Exalted Paladin; a Rogue learns Dread Blade from a death knight. Each act has 3–5 sites, and most are hidden off the
main path (a secret marker on the map once found). Each site's figure belongs to the era its act explores.

## Build order

Act by act, down the list. Each zone: plan → kit zone spec → exits both ways → world map → encounters and monsters
→ quest chain → test → captures. Zones without their own kit are greybox on the forest kit with a per-zone colour
grade, then re-dressed once the kit art is approved.
