Sources:
- **Narrative Bible**: `docs/AOTL_Seven_Act_Narrative_and_World_Design_Bible.docx`. The owner set it as the reference
  for every NPC, zone, enemy, storyline and quest name (11-28). Its 105 zone IDs (ACT01_Z01 … ACT07_Z15) and quest IDs
  (Q_A01_01 … Q_A07_15) are the build's keys.
- **World Atlas**: `docs/AOTL_World_Atlas_and_Environment_Bible_v1.docx`. It labels itself "proposed draft material,
  to be reviewed". The owner asked for it to be used to fill gaps (11-28).

Rule: **the bible decides names, story, cast, quests and zone order. The Atlas fills what the bible leaves open**:
environment language, art direction, sub-locations, weather, settlement and dungeon logic, and production sheets.

## What the Atlas adds (used)

1. **Era palettes** (its section 2.2), applied as each zone's biome grade until curated art exists:
   - Present: weathered stone, patched timber, blue-gold heraldry.
   - Golden Age: white-gold and ivory, luminous metal, broad sightlines.
   - Cataclysm: fire, fractures, chronal stormlight.
   - Unwritten: impossible seams, mixed fragments.
   - Chronicle Core: mythic machinery, voids, regulator pylons.
2. **Zone design rule** (every act): each main zone has
   - one hero vista,
   - one readable combat space,
   - one story-delivery space,
   - one environmental clue pointing to the past.

   Temporal variants change architecture, occupancy or infrastructure, not only colour. The builder's point-of-interest
   slots follow this rule: a landmark (vista), the boss or guardian clearing (combat), the NPC or lorecast spot
   (story), and a ruin or overlay clue (past).
3. **Sub-locations.** Atlas names that fit inside bible zones are used as their districts and landmarks:
   - **Crownsfall** (painted plaza, and its later districts): Gate District, Royal Market, Bluebanner Square,
     Bellfound Ward, Westgarden Terraces, Pilgrim's Rise. Highward Steps uses Pilgrim's Rise and Westgarden Terraces;
     the Citadel uses Upper Citadel Approach.
   - **Kingsroad Fields:** Old King's Road, Weeping Aqueduct, Dawnwatch Fields.
   - **Bramblewatch:** Greenmile Orchard.
   - **Greenwood Marches:** Brackenwood Verge.
   - **Mossbell Hamlet:** Hollowbrook.
   - **Undercroft and Cathedral:** Cathedral Underdeep, Sunken Archive.

   Later acts borrow Atlas landmark ideas in the same way: Widow's Span (bridge set piece) for Hearthstone Crossing,
   Watcher's End (lighthouse keep) for Greywake Coast, Fields of Glass for the First Sundering aftermath, Market of
   Borrowed Faces for Court of Borrowed Faces, Lantern Court for Keepers' Lantern, Covenant Port for Greywake Armada.
4. **Enemy families** (Atlas act notes + bible creature progression):
   - Act I: raiders, scarecrows, wolves, poachers, then crypt families.
   - Act II: frontier beasts, marsh horrors, weaponised-fracture factions.
   - Act III: Golden Age defenders and constructs.
   - Act IV: disaster and cult forces.
   - Act V: displaced epoch combatants.
   - Act VI: war creatures from the scars and Golden Age war machines.
   - Act VII: Engine avatars.
5. **Weather and atmosphere by region:**
   - frontier: mist;
   - Golden Age: brilliant clarity;
   - Cataclysm: ash and stormlight;
   - Unwritten: impossible skies;
   - Core: void.

   This sets each zone's mist and colour grade.
6. **Settlements per act.** Each act has a primary hub, a secondary refuge and one dead settlement. Act I: Crownsfall
   (hub), Bramblewatch (refuge), and Mossbell's haunted mill quarter (no longer functioning).
7. **Dungeons** come out of the geography (cathedral, cistern, vaults, observatories, forts), with exteriors visible
   from the zone outside where possible. Rare Lorecasting dungeons stay outside the 105 count (bible §24).
8. **Lorecasting environment rules.** Overlays reveal who used a space and why; some are stable and revisitable,
   others are unstable. Every lorecast site has a present state and a revealed state.
9. **Zone production sheet template** (Atlas section 6): the per-zone brief format for the art pass.
10. **Continental logic.** The west is the royal heartland (Crownsfall). The world opens outward through the frontier
    into preserved Golden Age corridors, the Cataclysm belt, the Unwritten margins, late-war deserts and the core.
    This is consistent with the bible's map: Kingsroad west, Veyr mountains, Greywake coast south-west, Riftglass
    north-east.

## Owner lore note 11-28: why "Crownsfall"

The city is named for **the crown that fell, and the empire that fell with it**. This matches the bible: Crownsfall
"rises on fragments of older cities" (present age, Year ~3,200), and Act I's reveal is that "Crownsfall contains older
city layers". In the environment:

- **The Fallen Crown.** A colossal toppled crown of the old empire, weathered ivory stone and tarnished gold, lies
  half-sunk at the foot of the great waterfall. The falls pour over and through its broken points. It is the city's
  namesake landmark (previously "Crown Falls" on the plan).
- **The lower tiers** (Bellfound Ward, Lantern Quays, the Gate District foundations) show the older imperial masonry
  under the present city: larger, finer, broken arches and columns with the newer timber-and-slate homes built on and
  into them.
- **Present-day Crownsfall** (blue-gold heraldry, ivory limestone, slate) is built on top. Dialogue and lorecasts can
  point at the crown without explaining the full truth before the bible allows it.
