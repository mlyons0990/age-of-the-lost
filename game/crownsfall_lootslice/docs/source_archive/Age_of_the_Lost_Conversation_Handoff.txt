## 4. Character development — chronological, condensed
**V1 — Royal Wayfarer initial playable asset.** Original animated GLB, ~43,334 triangles, 56 bones, 17 animation clips, distinct materials; Blender import script + Godot viewer. Offline structural checks; no Blender/Godot runtime validation by the assistant.

**V2 — Polished Royal Wayfarer.** More face/hair detail, slimmer shoulders, clothing/metal trim and Godot toon/PBR test. ~54,312 triangles; kept 56 bones and 17 clips.

**V3 — Crownsfall Royal Warrior.** Blue/gold royal costume closer to approved imagery, long cape and heraldry. Integrated into a copy of the user’s existing Godot project with reversible update patch and full copy. **User reported V3 working in Blender and Godot.**

**V4 — Costume/geometry refinement.** More layered hair, fitted outfit/armor, embroidered cape and sword details. ~112,118 triangles reported in final revision. Further offline geometry/deformation validation. **User reported V4 working.**

**V5 — Motion/articulation focus.** Corrected gait curves and GLB animation clip timing, improved knee/elbow weights, synced separate Godot-authored pose JSON, velocity-based motion logic and finger/toe articulation. Example reported improvement: sprint ankle height during stance reduced from ~242 mm (V4) to ~8 mm (V5) in offline sampling; not conclusive evidence of zero in-engine foot slide. **User explicitly said V5 tested perfectly in Blender and Godot.** Treat V5 as the reliable motion baseline.

**V6–V10 — Artistic Blender scripts, model layers and painterly texture passes.** Locked the Royal Wayfarer concept sheet as primary reference. Added hair crown/fringe locks, face accents, shaped pauldrons/collar, cape liner, tabards, greaves, belt/embroidery layers and generated fabric/steel/gold textures. Many deliveries were code and textures awaiting PC execution, not pre-rendered final production models. **User explicitly confirmed V8, V9 and V10 worked in Blender/Godot**; preserve those practical baselines.

**V11 — Surface art applied to actual GLB via UVs.** Most important material pipeline correction: identified existing per-surface 0–1 UV regions, created distinct cape crest and tabard embroidery maps, and embedded textures in real GLB material slots. Two branches:
  1. Ready importable `AgeLost_Royal_Wayfarer_V11_UV_Textured_Base.glb`, based on V5 geometry with new UV material bindings. **Does not include V6–V10 Blender-only geometry overlays.** Independently loaded offline; documented 56 joints, 17 clips, 28 updated material slots and 14 embedded texture images.
  2. `V11_Build_Full_Crownsfall_Warrior_Blender.py` rebuilds the cumulative additional details on top of UV-textured base and exports `AgeLost_Royal_Wayfarer_V11_SurfaceArt_FULL.glb` and editable `.blend`. This full variant required Blender execution and Godot verification. User did not explicitly report V11 as fully tested in both programs.

**Persistent limitation:** Even after V11, real mesh face/hair/anatomy/materials do not fully resemble the beautifully painted character sheet. Needs genuine sculpting/retopology, intentional hair forms, and tested toon/painterly look-development instead of more additive overlays.

## 8. Critical lessons and mistakes to avoid
- **Do not create another flat primitive/blockout scene and announce it as “2026/AAA quality.”** The user explicitly rejected that approach multiple times.
- **Do not confuse character concept-art generation with creation of usable 3D geometry.** A beautiful generated portrait/sheet is a style reference, not a `.blend` sculpt or animated `.glb`.
- **Do not claim a rendering or tested Godot playthrough without actually executing and checking it.** Explicitly label CPU geometry previews vs Blender renders vs Godot gameplay recordings.
- Do not just increment version number or pass count. Demonstrate real improvements on the correct scene/assets, from a consistent in-engine screenshot matching the locked reference composition.
- Preserve the actual already working 56-bone character rig, motion and combat while rebuilding environment aesthetics.
- Avoid repeated layered “floating” costume ornaments and overlapping cape emblems; use correct UV/texturing and well-modeled underlying geometry.
- Avoid duplicating a full environment through source GLB and a procedural version in one scene. Keep one authoritative environment mesh and collision set with a clear source path.
- Check stairs, collisions, clipping, terrain walkability, occlusion, animation states, shader and performance after every major change.
